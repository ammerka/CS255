# CS255
### CS 255 DriverPass Portfolio

This repository contains the documents that I created for my projects this semester. In Project One, I reviewed the DriverPass interview transcript and created a business requirements document. In Project Two, I used the client's requirements to create a system design document.

DriverPass is a driving school that helps people prepare for their DMV driving tests. They wanted a web-based system to manage driving lessons and online training. DriverPass wanted their customers to be able to sign up for different packages that provide access to varying services. These services include booking driving lessons and having access to online classes and practice DMV tests. DriverPass also wanted their staff to be able to manage their customers and have tools to help them run their business (ie: manage instructors, vehicles, and appointments).

Some things that I think went particularly well for me were incorporating DriverPass' needs in my design and thinking about all the different types of users that will be using the system and what they each have access to. I also thought about things like making sure that when a lesson is booked the instructor, customer, and car are all available for that time slot. Even though the customer and instructor can technically be available, only one lesson can use a certain car at a given time.

If I had the chance to go back and revise part of my design, I think that the section that I would change is the technical requirements. I think that some of the wording got more complex than needed and could have been written in a simpler manner. Shortening some explanations and emphasizing what DriverPass specifically needs/wants and why would make this section clearer. It would also be helpful to make a distinction between requirements that DriverPass specifically mentioned and requirements/suggestions that I came up with but still need DriverPass' approval.

In the DriverPass interview transcript and through my business requirements document, I learned what DriverPass' customers and staff needed to be able to do. I tried to incorporate these requirements into my design by using diagrams to show what actions the users can take, the process for performing those actions, and what data is needed for/affected by those actions. For example, through my use case diagrams, activity diagrams, sequence diagrams, and class diagrams, I was able to design the process for booking a driving lesson that matches up a customer with an available instructor and car during a time slot that works for everyone. It is important to incorporate the end user's needs in a design because no matter how aesthetically pleasing a system is, if the end users find it difficult to use then it isn't solving the intended problem.

Personally, when it comes to designing a system I like to first listen to the client's wants/needs and ask as many questions as possible before trying to decide how the system should work. From there, I like to list out as many requirements as possible, identify the different types of users, and break the system down into its different parts. Diagrams are very helpful in fleshing out the processes in a system and can help identify areas that haven't been fully thought out. Going forward, I would like to incorporate this same process but also get client/user feedback sooner instead of only at the end. I would also like to try thinking of more scenarios such as what happens if two people try to book the same time or what happens if a customer runs out of available lesson hours. Lastly, I would like to work on making my diagrams more concise while still being descriptive. I don't want to end up in a situation where someone needs a diagram in order to understand my diagram.
